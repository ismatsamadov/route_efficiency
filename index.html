All routes and carriers

Every route the map carries, with what it measures, and the same figures added up for each carrier that runs them. Sort by any column to find the most roundabout routes, the longest detours or the routes that depend on no one else.

How it was measured

Routes, stops and route lines come from AYNA's public map API, taken on ; the data is identical, row for row, to a copy taken on . Defects in the map data were detected and repaired automatically, as listed above. Residents come from the Kontur population grid (November 2023, hexagons of about 0.74 km²) and boardings from the daily counts BakuBus publishes on opendata.az (, weekday average).

Direct route
In both directions the path is at most 1.4 times the straight line between the end stops. Indirect: 1.4 to 2 times, very indirect: 2 to 3 times, loop: more than 3 times, or ends less than 1 km apart.
Detour
A stretch where the bus travels at least 2.5 times the straight line between two of its stops, and at least 1.5 km more (12 stops at most). The extra minutes are estimates from speeds assumed by type of service.
Shorter path
The shortest way between a detour's two ends along the links between stops that buses drive today, as the paper's redesign search finds it. It is shown when it is at least 0.5 km shorter; the minutes it saves are estimates, counting 20 seconds for every stop it skips.
Near-duplicate
At least 85% of the stops of a route with 10 or more stops are also served by one other route. Express routes are left out: skipping stops is what they are for.
Corridor
A stretch of street of at least 1 km where every link between consecutive stops carries at least 8 routes.
Stop spacing
A stop could go when, on every route that uses it, it lies less than 250 m along the route from a neighbouring stop and removing it leaves no gap over 700 m. Terminals and interchange hubs are kept, and stops served by fewer routes go first. This is the stop-balancing scenario of the paper.
Reach
The stops on the routes that stop at a given stop; with one change, also the stops of every route that stops within 300 m of those. It counts connections, not minutes.
Travel time
Estimated minutes by bus from a stop, from the paper's travel model: half a route's headway of waiting at each boarding, the ride at speeds set by type of service with 20 seconds lost at each stop, and walks of up to 300 m between stops to change buses, which are avoided as if each change cost 5 minutes. Headways are estimated for 200 of the 201 routes, so the minutes are estimates.
Carriers
The routes each carrier runs, as AYNA's map assigns them, with their figures added up. AYNA plans the routes, so the figures describe the routes, not how well a carrier runs them. Names that differ only in spelling are counted once, which gives the paper's 39 carriers.
Coverage
A resident lives less than 400 m in a straight line from the nearest stop. Each hexagon is split into seven parts so that coverage is not judged from its centre alone.

Everything rests on the geometry of the map: timetables, vehicle positions and where riders travel are not public. The map does not yet show the new network that has been launching since January 2026, so the results describe the network the map shows today.

The paper also includes a search that redesigns the network with the same number of buses. Its result rests on estimated headways and a demand model, so only its shorter paths, which need neither, are shown here; the paper gives the rest with its conditions.
