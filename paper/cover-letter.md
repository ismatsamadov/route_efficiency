To the Editor-in-Chief, Case Studies on Transport Policy

Dear Editor,

I submit the article "How efficient are Baku's bus routes? An open-data measurement and
a fleet-neutral redesign" for consideration in Case Studies on Transport Policy.

Baku, a city of 2.4 million, is redesigning its network of about 200 bus routes, but no
evaluation of the network has been published and the data that one would normally rest
on (timetables, vehicle positions, where riders travel) is not public. The paper asks
how much can be measured, and how far a redesign can be tested, from public data alone,
and answers with three results. First, the inherited network is measured from the
agency's public map after its undocumented defects are repaired: only 29 of 201 routes
are direct, 271 detours add 939 km to one trip in each direction of every route, and
66% of residents live within 400 m of a stop. Second, a door-to-door travel model built
from public data ranks routes against observed boardings at a Spearman correlation of
0.58, and the paper says so rather than assuming the model is right. Third, a
fleet-neutral redesign search shortens the average trip by about a minute or gives
today's modelled travel times with about 12% fewer buses, a gain that holds on 10
independent trip samples and under 14 alternative assumptions, including a run without
the least certain kind of change.

The paper fits the journal's remit as a case study whose lessons travel: many cities
publish a map and little else, and the pipeline, every input and every result are
released with a DOI (10.5281/zenodo.23604370) so that the measurements can be
regenerated and the method applied elsewhere once a reader for another map format is
written. The manuscript is about 6,600 words with four tables and four figures. It is
not under consideration elsewhere and has not been published before; a preprint was
submitted to engrXiv on 24 October 2026 (submission 8632) and its DOI will be given on
the title page once assigned. I have no competing interests and
received no funding. I am the sole author, writing as an independent researcher. The
earlier, unreviewed GitHub report on the same data that the manuscript cites and
supersedes is my own.

Suggested reviewers are entered in the submission system; none has any connection to
me.
