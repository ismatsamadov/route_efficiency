# From the repository to a paper

My plan for turning this work into a research paper as an independent researcher:
whether there is a paper here, what it should contain, where I could publish it without
paying, what format journals expect, and the steps from this repository to a submitted
manuscript. I read every fact about a journal or publisher below on its own pages on
23 October 2026; fees and policies change, so I will re-check the page before
submitting.

## 1. Is there a paper here?

I think so: a case-study and planning-support paper. Its contribution is not a new
algorithm but a complete, reproducible pipeline that measures a real bus network from
public data alone, estimates what a fleet-neutral redesign would gain, and quantifies
how much that estimate depends on its own assumptions. Four things in the repository
are publishable: the repaired network and its measured diagnosis
([results.md](results.md#todays-network)); the door-to-door travel model built only
from public data, with its fit against observed ridership stated honestly (0.58 fair,
0.77 circular; [fit ladder](results.md#the-demand-model-as-each-input-is-added)); the
redesign, 51.8 → 50.9 minutes with the same buses or today's travel times with about
12% fewer buses, robust over ten trip samples and eleven alternative assumptions
([robustness](results.md#how-robust-the-results-are)), including the finding that the
strict coverage guard costs nothing; and the pipeline itself, with every input
committed, every output regenerated byte for byte, tests, and every assumption in one
settings file.

What it is not, and what the paper must say plainly: not a forecast (headways and
demand are estimates), not a methods contribution (a greedy search with no benchmark
comparison), and a description of the network as AYNA's map API publishes it, which was
the same on 23 October 2026 as on 6 May 2026 even though AYNA has been launching a new
network since January 2026 ([data.md](data.md#when-the-data-is-from)): the map lags the
street.

One thing reviewers will find if they search my name: my earlier GitHub report *Baku
Public Transit Network Optimization: Strategic Analysis & Implementation Roadmap*
(github.com/analisto/ayna_gov_az, February 2026, under the byline "Transport Systems
Research Group") uses the same AYNA data but different definitions and different
numbers (an "efficiency score" of 62.73/100, 46.84% overlap, 41.89% "resource waste",
dollar savings). The manuscript cites it in section 2.1 and says that this work
supersedes it with defined, reproducible measures, and the README of that repository
now points here.

## 2. Three ways forward

The first is to write the case-study paper now. The repository already contains
everything it needs: 6,000–7,000 words along the outline in section 3, submitted to
one of the venues in section 4. I expect two to four weeks of writing and a week for
figures and statements.

The second is to strengthen the work first, for a stronger venue. Each of these is two
to six weeks: a rerun on a new AYNA snapshot once the map API changes (on 23 October
2026 it still returned the May data), which would show which findings survive the
network AYNA is launching and turn the snapshot into a before-and-after comparison;
the metro in the travel graph, the most obvious model gap
for Baku; vehicle capacity in the frequency setting, which answers the "you overload
busy routes" objection; calibration of the demand model with routes held out, which
would raise the fair 0.58 and make the validation standard; and the evaluator on the
Mandl and Mumford benchmarks, needed only if I claim anything about the search method.
All of them are in [roadmap.md](roadmap.md#what-i-would-do-next).

The third is a pivot that reuses the pipeline. The strongest is a before-and-after
study of Baku's 2026 network reform: AYNA's new network is rolling out now, measuring
the old network (this snapshot) and the new one with the same pipeline is a natural
experiment few cities offer, and it needs only a second snapshot, as soon as the map API
shows the new network, and the counts for the same months. Others: an accessibility and equity paper (who is within reach of a stop,
by district and by travel time, before and after the redesign; the population grid,
the coverage curve and the door-to-door model already support it, and it fits the
Journal of Transport Geography or the Journal of Transport and Land Use); a data and
software paper, with the repaired AYNA snapshot and the first GTFS feeds for Baku
(`output/redesign/gtfs_today.zip`) as a dataset with a Zenodo DOI and `busnet` as
research software in the Journal of Open Source Software, which is free and asks for an
open licence, tests and documentation, all of which exist; a short Findings paper on one
result, for example that the strict coverage guard gains as much as the default, in
1,000 words and three figures.

## 5. Format

The structure is numbered sections (1, 1.1); an introduction that states the gap and
the objectives without a long literature survey; methods detailed enough to reproduce;
results without interpretation; discussion with the limitations; conclusions;
appendices A, B with Table A.1 and so on; acknowledgements and declarations before the
references (Elsevier) or on the title page (double-anonymised journals).

Lengths: Case Studies on Transport Policy under 7,000 words; Journal of Transport
Geography 4,000–6,000; Periodica Polytechnica 8 pages; Transactions on Transport
Sciences 15 pages; TRB Annual Meeting 20 pages; Findings 1,000 words; Public Transport
and TRR direct submissions have no limit.

References are author-year in every venue above (APA 7 at JTLU, TOTS and Findings;
Chicago author-date at TRR from 30 June 2026; Elsevier applies its own style at proof),
with DOIs. Datasets are cited as data: author, year, title, repository, version, DOI,
with "[dataset]" where Elsevier asks for it.

Templates: on Overleaf, *Elsevier Article (elsarticle)* in its name-year variant for the
Elsevier journals, the *Springer Nature LaTeX Template* (sn-jnl, author-year option)
for Public Transport, the *Findings Template*, and an unofficial TRB template for the
Annual Meeting, to be checked against the current rules. Word is accepted everywhere;
JTLU requires its Word template after acceptance. Pandoc or Quarto can produce either
from Markdown, so the docs here can be the first draft.

Figures: vector (PDF, EPS, SVG) for charts and maps, 300 dpi for raster, Arial or
Helvetica lettering, readable in grey, one file per figure, numbered, no screenshots,
and OpenStreetMap attribution on every map made from its data.

Required statements: data availability (with the Zenodo DOI), code availability,
competing interests ("none"), funding ("no funding was received"), author contributions
(CRediT roles at Elsevier), ethics (section 6), and generative-AI use (JTLU and TRB
require disclosure; TRB counts rewriting with AI tools). Elsevier journals also want 3–5
highlights.

The mistakes editors say they reject for: opening with a trite sentence about
congestion; an abstract that repeats the introduction; purely descriptive content with
no lesson; undefined acronyms in the abstract; PDF as the source file; a missing data
statement or highlights; tables as images; figures inside the Word file; incomplete
references; undisclosed AI use; submitting to a journal while the same paper is under
TRB review.

## 7. Step by step

1. Repository: note at the top of the February 2026 GitHub report that this work
   supersedes it (`LICENSE`, `CITATION.cff`, the Licensing section of the README and the
   Zenodo DOI of release v1.1.0 are in place).
2. Decide between the three ways forward in section 2. For the paper as it is, state
   that the map API was unchanged between May and October 2026 and list the
   before-and-after comparison as future work.
3. Write the paper from section 3, 6,000–7,000 words, figures from `output/`. The draft
   is in [`paper/manuscript.md`](../paper/manuscript.md), written as the anonymised
   review copy for Case Studies on Transport Policy, with the author details and the
   identifying declarations in [`paper/title-page.md`](../paper/title-page.md); every
   number in it was checked against `output/`, and `python3 paper/figures.py` draws its
   four figures as SVG and PDF into `paper/figures/` from the committed outputs, with the
   standard library only; `python3 paper/check.py` reports the word counts, the
   length of each highlight and whether every citation has a reference entry, and
   `--wrap` re-wraps the prose. Ask two readers (a transport planner and a quantitative researcher) to
   comment.
4. Post a preprint: engrXiv (no endorsement needed), or arXiv physics.soc-ph with an
   endorsement; record the DOI in the README, on the title page and in ORCID. The file
   to upload is `paper/submission/preprint.pdf` (the full paper with the author line
   and the figures, built by `python3 paper/build.py`); Elsevier's sharing policy allows
   a preprint on any server at any time, so posting it before submission is fine.
5. Submit, in this order of fit and cost: Public Transport (subscription option, free;
   submitted on 24 October 2026 as a Case Study and Application through its Editorial
   Manager site, provisional number PUTR-S-26-00312; the system's built PDF is kept at
   `paper/submission/PUTR-S-26-00312.pdf`) or Case Studies on Transport Policy (subscription option, free); if a free open-access
   paper matters more than the journal's standing, Periodica Polytechnica Transportation
   Engineering or Transactions on Transport Sciences. Send one sharp result to Findings
   at the same time only if the journals allow it.
6. Prepare the package: cover letter (aims, three findings, fit, novelty, not under
   review elsewhere, preprint DOI, Zenodo DOI), title page with affiliation and ORCID,
   anonymised manuscript where required, highlights, declarations, data statement.
   `python3 paper/build.py` writes all of it to `paper/submission/`: `manuscript.docx`
   (the anonymised review copy), `title-page.docx`, `highlights.docx`,
   `cover-letter.docx` (from `paper/cover-letter.md`) and `preprint.pdf`, with the
   figures to upload separately from `paper/figures/figure-1.pdf` to `figure-4.pdf`.
   The reviewers to suggest are in `paper/reviewers.md`, and
   `paper/submission/fields.md` is a paste sheet with the exact value of every form
   field for engrXiv and Editorial Manager. What remains is done in the journal's
   Editorial Manager with my own account: upload the files, enter the
   reviewers with their institutional e-mails, add the preprint DOI to the title page
   and the cover letter, and answer the submission questionnaire.
7. After acceptance: add the reference to the preprint, update `CITATION.cff` with
   `preferred-citation`, make a new release if anything changed, add the paper to ORCID.
